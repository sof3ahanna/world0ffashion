HAUTE COUTURE

"COUTURIER" AND "HIGH FASHION" REDIRECT HERE. HAUTE COUTURE IS THE CREATION OF EXCLUSIVE CUSTOM-FITTED HIGH-END FASHION DESIGN. BEGINNING IN THE MID-NINETEENTH CENTURY, PARIS BECAME THE CENTRE OF A GROWING INDUSTRY THAT FOCUSED ON MAKING OUTFITS FROM HIGH-QUALITY, EXPENSIVE, OFTEN UNUSUAL FABRIC AND SEWN WITH EXTREME ATTENTION TO DETAIL AND FINISHED BY THE MOST EXPERIENCED AND CAPABLE OF SEWERS.

The term haute couture is protected by French law and defined by the Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture. Their rules state that only those companies mentioned on the list drawn up each year by a commission domiciled at the Ministry for Industry are entitled to avail themselves of the label.

The Chambre Syndicale de la Couture Parisienne is an association of Parisian couturiers founded in 1868 as an outgrowth of medieval guilds that regulate its members with regard to counterfeiting of styles, dates of openings for collections, number of models presented, and promotional activities.

HISTORY

Haute couture can reference roots as early as the 17th century. High fashion and garment power were legally protected through strict decrees that required strict adherence to quality, quantity, and luxury showcase settings. Guild arrangements acquired the rights to tailor clothing for women and children, while male tailors held the right to make clothing for men and boys.
